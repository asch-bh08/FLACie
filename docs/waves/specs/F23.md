# F23 Spotify's playlists, downloaded as you go

## Summary

"Playlists for you" shows Spotify's real playlists (This Is <artist>, All Out 90s, Rock Classics, Today's Top Hits, genres), read with no key
from Spotify's public embed pages (open.spotify.com/embed/playlist/<id>, the __NEXT_DATA__ JSON: name, track titles and artists), refreshed
about daily on frank. Only the track LISTS are kept (a few KB each); songs are matched to the library like Import. Songs not in the library are
downloaded only as interest shows, never all at once (every playlist in full would be terabytes):
- Opening or playing a playlist (or a song from it): download all its missing songs, in play order, the next few first.
- Searching an artist: download that artist's This Is playlist's missing songs, top half first, the rest after.
- Popular lists (charts, and decades and genres the person listens to): only the first 2 missing songs each, so they start instantly.
- No daily cap. When the music disk has under 100 GB free: all automatic downloads stop (a single song the person asks for still downloads),
  and a clean-up runs: exact duplicates, lower-quality copies of a song that has a better copy, and files that fail to decode. Clean-up moves
  files to a holding folder on the same disk that is emptied after 30 days, so a wrong removal can be undone; Server admin lists what it moved.
- Search never floods: the All view shows at most 3 playlists (best match first). A "Playlists" filter shows them all, and inside it a genre
  filter (Pop, Rock, Hip-Hop, Dance... from the library's genres): searching "pop" with Playlists shows every pop playlist, best first.
- SoundCloud playlists (through yt-dlp) appear too, ranked not by a fixed number but relative to the others found for the same search: by likes,
  plays and reposts per day since posted, the artist's or label's own verified account first; one under 1,000 likes or in the bottom half of its
  search's results is never shown; they
  follow the same as-you-go download rules as Spotify's.
- One list per purpose: an artist's best-of, a decade, a genre each come from ONE source, in order Spotify > Deezer > SoundCloud > our own. So
  with Spotify's This Is The Prodigy there is no SoundCloud or Deezer Prodigy best-of. SoundCloud fills what Spotify lacks: remix sets,
  bootleg and edit collections, DJ mixes, label sets.
- The embed page lists at most 100 songs; longer Spotify playlists use their first 100.
Decades, genres and charts come from a fixed list of Spotify playlist IDs; an artist's This Is playlist is found through Spotify's own search
when possible, else Deezer's equivalent ("100% <artist>"), else FLACie's own Best of. Our own lists stay as the fallback, fixed to one version
per song (no instrumentals; album version preferred) and sized to the artist (half their distinct songs, 10 to 50), hits first.
